The golf things nobody tells you

The things everyone expects you to know, but nobody explains.

Golf has safety habits, etiquette, little conventions and ways of looking after the course that are easy to miss when you’re new. Here are ten to make your first few rounds easier.

Your tee time is when your group starts playing.

Arrive early enough to park, check in, sort your gear and get to the first tee ready. Check the course’s arrival requirements when you book.

Choose tees for your ability, not your ego.

The right tees mean fewer big carries, fewer lost balls and less waiting, and a much more enjoyable round.

Know where to stand when someone is hitting.

Stand safely off to the side and slightly behind, out of the swing, the ball’s path and the player’s eyeline.

Watch every tee shot.

Watch the ball all the way down and pick a spot to remember where it landed. Four sets of eyes make balls much easier to find.

“FORE!” means now.

Call it straight away if a ball might head towards someone. Don’t wait to see if it gets there.

Ready golf does not mean rushed golf.

Get ready while others are playing, then go when it’s safe and it’s your turn, without rushing or distracting anyone.

Why are golfers carrying sand?

Many Australian courses provide sand or sand-and-seed to fill divot marks. Follow the course’s signs or local instructions.

What is the invisible line everyone avoids?

A putting line is the path the ball will roll from where it is to the hole. Walk around it, not across it, and think about where the next putt might go.

Par is not necessarily your target.

Par is what a scratch golfer is expected to score. Your ability and handicap change what a good result looks like for you.

The flag is not always the target.

If the flag is close to a bunker, an edge or a steep slope, the middle of the green is often the smarter target.

There is much more to golf than the swing.

We’ll keep explaining the unwritten parts of golf, one useful thing at a time.
